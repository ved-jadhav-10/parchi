## 5. CDSCO Medical Device Software (MDSW) guidance and clinical decision support

### Takeaway
CORRECTED/VERIFIED: the draft was October 2025. The final guidance was issued by circular dated 21 Jul 2026, Doc No. CDSCO/MD/GD/MDSW/01/2026, under the Medical Devices Rules, 2017. Standalone software is classed with an IMDRF-style matrix. Software that "informs clinical management" sits in the lowest column: Class A for serious conditions, Class B for critical conditions. Software that "drives" management or "treats/diagnoses" sits higher. However, modules that recommend drug dosages are named as regulated MDSW, not exempt IT.

### Cited Findings
- The final guidance was issued by circular dated 21 Jul 2026, Doc No. CDSCO/MD/GD/MDSW/01/2026, following the Oct 2025 draft. It describes itself as interpretive guidance, "not a new regulatory control". — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/code-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance); [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=0049fff8-5f9c-469c-8701-0a30b009a4c5)
- Risk classes run A (low) to D (high). Software that drives hardware takes the hardware's class. Standalone software is weighed by "significance of the information provided for healthcare decision-making" against the healthcare situation (critical, serious or non-serious). — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/code-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance)
- Table 2 matrix, as reproduced by a secondary source:

  | Situation | Treat or diagnose | Drive clinical management | Inform clinical management |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Critical | D | C | B |
  | Serious | C | B | A |
  | Non-serious | B | A | A |

  "Inform" covers software that aggregates or presents reference data, guidelines or clinical information to help clinicians. LIS/EHR modules that "recommend drug dosages" are regulated MDSW, and treatment-dosing optimisation counts as "drive clinical management". — [MedDeviceGuide (vendor blog, secondary)](https://meddeviceguide.com/blog/india-cdsco-medical-device-software-mdsw-guidance-2026-guide)
- Exclusions: software solely for wellness, education or training; software only for data transfer, storage, archival, formatting or communication; and hospital, lab or clinical information system functions with no independent medical purpose. The exclusion is function-specific. — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/code-compliance-decoding-indias-medical-device-software-guidance)
- Sources conflict on terminology. One law commentary says the final guidance drops the draft's SaMD/SiMD split; another says it keeps it. — [search summary of NatLawReview and Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=0049fff8-5f9c-469c-8701-0a30b009a4c5)

### Inferences
- Yes, "informs a professional" lowers the class. If Parchi flags issues for a pharmacist, who makes the decision, it fits "inform clinical management": likely Class A (serious) or B (critical) if it is MDSW at all. It is not "drive" or "diagnose".
- The risk is that overdose and max-dose checks could be read as "recommending dosages", which the guidance names as regulated. Keep the UI wording strictly informational ("exceeds the reference max of X mg/day; source Y; pharmacist to review"), with no auto-corrected dose. This matches the CLAUDE.md rule that output informs and never decides.
- Deck wording: "Designed as decision support that informs a pharmacist, the lowest-risk tier in CDSCO's MDSW guidance (Jul 2026)." Do not claim "exempt" or "not a medical device".

### Gaps
- The CDSCO PDF itself was not opened. The matrix comes from a vendor blog, so confirm Table 2 on cdsco.gov.in before printing class letters on a slide.
- I found no explicit CDSCO example for drug-interaction or duplicate-ingredient checkers.

## 6. DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025 (on-device data)

### Takeaway
The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 with a phased rollout. Consent-manager provisions start at about 1 year (Nov 2026). Most data fiduciary obligations apply after 18 months (about 13 May 2027). The highest penalty, up to Rs 250 crore, applies to failing to take reasonable security safeguards against a breach (Section 8(5)). Keeping patient data on-device shrinks what Parchi as a vendor ever holds.

### Cited Findings
- The Rules were notified in Nov 2025. Rules 1, 2 and 17-21 apply immediately, Rule 4 (consent managers) after 1 year, and Rules 3, 5-16 and 22-23 after 18 months (one law firm puts this at 13 May 2027). — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fcentre-notifies-dpdp-rules-implementation-planned-in-phases-spread-over-12-18-months-3798465); [PIB backgrounder, 17 Nov 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/breaking-govt-notifies-final-dpdp-rules-sets-staggered-rollout-with-key-obligations-for-data-fiduciaries-84201.htm)
- Penalty ceilings: up to Rs 250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards (Section 8(5)); up to Rs 200 crore for failing to notify a breach (Section 8(6)). These are "may extend to" ceilings. — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/view-point/companies-can-save-up-to-250-crore-navigating-the-dpdp-act-2023)
- The Rules carve out healthcare processing of children's data from the verifiable parental consent requirement. — [search summary of Mondaq / The Leaflet](https://theleaflet.in/explainer/indias-new-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-a-detailed-reading)

### Inferences
- The DPDP Act has no separate "sensitive health data" category in the search results reviewed. Health data is ordinary personal data, but breaches carry the highest penalty band.
- If Parchi stores prescriptions only on the pharmacist's device and never sends them to Parchi's servers, Parchi does not act as a data processor for that data. The pharmacy remains the data fiduciary for what it enters. Optional online LLM parsing via `api/` would send text off-device, so it should strip patient identifiers and be opt-in.
- Deck wording: "Patient data never leaves the device: built for India's DPDP Act, where security lapses carry penalties up to Rs 250 crore."

### Gaps
- The MeitY primary text was not retrieved (the PIB PDF was unreadable). The exact notification date (13 or 14 Nov 2025) is not confirmed from primary text.
- The personal/domestic-use exclusion (Section 3) was not verified in this session.

## 7. Registered pharmacist requirement and e-pharmacy rules

### Takeaway
VERIFIED: Section 42 of the Pharmacy Act 1948 allows only a registered pharmacist to compound, prepare, mix or dispense medicine on a prescription; it has been in force nationally since 1984. Rule 65 of the Drugs Rules 1945 requires supply by, or under the personal supervision of, a registered pharmacist. The e-pharmacy rules have stayed in draft since 28 Aug 2018. In 2026 there are further draft Rule 65 amendments, including CCTV, but none is final.

### Cited Findings
- Section 42 text: no person other than a registered pharmacist shall compound, prepare, mix or dispense any medicine on a medical practitioner's prescription. There are exceptions for practitioners dispensing to their own patients. — [Pharmacy Council of India](https://www.pci.nic.in/pdf/3.pdf); [AdvocateKhoj bare act](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/pharmacy/42.php)
- Section 42 came into effect in 1984; the 1976 amendment set a default 8-year commencement. The penalty is up to 6 months' imprisonment or a fine (historically Rs 1,000). Scroll reports the Jan Vishwas Bill altered pharmacy penalties; details are unverified. — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1053617/the-jan-vishwas-bill-passed-by-lok-sabha-further-dilutes-the-regulation-of-pharmacies)
- Rule 65(2) (Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945): licensees must have full-time registered pharmacists. This is cited in a state drug-control directive. — [Morung Express (Nagaland DCA directive)](https://morungexpress.com/drugs-license-holders-asked-to-comply-with-rules)
- E-pharmacy: the draft rules were notified on 28 Aug 2018 and remain a draft (about 8 years later). The draft required a registered pharmacist for dispensing and helpline queries. — [Novojuris](https://www.novojuris.com/thought-leadership/draft-rules-for-operation-of-e-pharmacies-in-india.html); [Spice Route Legal](https://spiceroutelegal.com/publications/regulation-of-e-pharmacies-in-india/)
- 2026 drafts: GSR 791(E) of 8 Sep 2026 proposes Rule 65(2A), which would require CCTV over prescription-drug sales at retail premises with 3 months' retention. GSR 861(E) of 28 Sep 2026 reportedly proposes curbs on advertising Schedule H, H1 and X drugs; this is single-source. Both are drafts. — [Prime Legal blog](https://blog.primelegal.in/draft-drug-rules-2026-cctv-mandate-pharmacies-india/)
- AIOCD called a nationwide chemist shutdown on 20 May 2026 against e-pharmacy notifications. — [Zee Biz](https://www.zeebiz.com/india/news-india-chemists-to-shut-shops-on-may-20-aiocd-calls-nationwide-strike-over-e-pharmacy-and-pricing-concerns-395390)

### Inferences
- The law already puts a registered pharmacist at the counter as the accountable decision-maker. Parchi supports that legally required role rather than replacing it, which is a strong feasibility and positioning point.

### Gaps
- The current Rule 65 text was not fetched from the official source. The GSR numbers for the 2026 drafts come from one law blog and were not checked against egazette.

## 8. NABH medication management

### Takeaway
VERIFIED (secondary): the NABH Hospital Accreditation Standards, 6th edition (effective 1 Jan 2025), has Chapter 3, "Management of Medication (MOM)". It covers prescribing, dispensing, high-risk and look-alike/sound-alike drugs, and adverse drug event reporting.

### Cited Findings
- 6th edition, Chapter 3, Management of Medication (MOM). It covers procurement, storage, prescribing, dispensing, administration and adverse-effect monitoring, with emphasis on high-risk drugs, narcotics and LASA medicines, standardised prescriptions, and reporting of medication errors and adverse drug events. — [NABH training page](https://nabh.co/training/hco-full-accreditation-masterclass-6th-edition-chapter-3-management-of-medication-mom/)
- The 6th edition is effective 1 Jan 2025, with 10 chapters and 639 objective elements, per a commercial course listing. — [Udemy listing](https://www.udemy.com/course/nabh-quality-management-certificate/)
