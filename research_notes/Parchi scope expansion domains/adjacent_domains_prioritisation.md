# Adjacent domains that would most strengthen Parchi's problem statement and business case (India, as of 25 Oct 2026)

Scope note: this file excludes handwritten-Rx OCR, CSV onboarding, allergies/eye drops, supplements, AYUSH and special populations (other researchers). "Parchi already has" facts come from the repo (`docs/PLAN.md` status rows, `docs/BUSINESS_MODEL.md`, `docs/PS_ANALYSIS.md` §3/§9). Repo check: Track A's existing `schedule_hint` is a dosing-frequency flag (weekly/monthly drugs such as methotrexate), not a regulatory schedule tag (`data/rxdata/schedule.py`, `data/curated/schedule_hints.csv`). So no Schedule H1/X/NDPS tagging exists yet. Phase 2 already has dated history windows, cross-doctor alerts and "refill smoothing" (same composition, same doctor, 7-day grace, unverified) (`docs/track-c-engine.md`).

## Key Question 1: Evidence, regulatory hook, buyer value, data, engine fit and build effort for each domain

### Takeaway
The strongest domains share one legal hook: **Schedule H1** of the Drugs Rules. H1 covers three groups at once: habit-forming drugs (alprazolam, codeine and others, with pregabalin added from **12 Nov 2026**), 3rd/4th-generation antibiotics, and anti-TB drugs. Each sale needs a register. Anti-TB dispensing also carries a legal duty to notify the patient to NTEP. These checks use data the buyer already holds (cart, order history), are deterministic, can be built in hours, and give e-pharmacies a compliance reason to buy at a time when e-pharmacy rules are still unenacted. Clinical "stacking" checks (QT, hypoglycaemia, bleeding, hyperkalaemia, serotonin) are the best clinical-impact add-on. Pharmacogenomics, food/fasting, cold chain, price and veterinary are weak or distracting for this pitch.

### Cited Findings

#### A. Abuse-prone and regulated drugs (Schedule H1, Schedule X, NDPS); early refill and doctor shopping across orders
- **Pregabalin moves into Schedule H1 on 12 Nov 2026.** Notification G.S.R. 377(E) dated 13 May 2026 adds it to Schedule H1. The amendment takes effect on 12 November 2026, and pregabalin "was not previously covered under any Schedule" — [Medical Dialogues / PTI, 16 Oct 2026](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/news/industry/pharma/punjab-seizes-rs-2064-crore-worth-of-medicines-suspends-520-chemist-licences-in-drug-law-crackdown-181441). The amendment was published in the Gazette of India Extraordinary on 20 May 2026. H1 means a prescription from a registered medical practitioner, a dedicated register for every sale, and a prominent pack warning. Authorities cited misuse among young people at doses "well above the therapeutic range" and seizures of illegal stock in the past year — [The Week, 22 May 2026](https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2026/05/22/pregabalin-schedule-h1-india.amp.html). The proposal was first reported in Jan 2026 — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/pregabalin-to-come-under-stricter-prescription-control-as-govt-proposes-schedule-h1-curbs-163648).
- The Week also cites evidence of gabapentinoid misuse: 1.6% prevalence in the general population and 3-68% among people with a history of opioid abuse (Evoy et al., *Drugs* 2017). It notes Punjab as an area of concern in peer-reviewed studies — [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/health/2026/05/22/pregabalin-schedule-h1-india.amp.html).
- **Punjab enforcement, Apr 2025 to Sep 2026:** 16,788 inspections, Rs 20.64 crore of medicines seized from 214 firms, **520 chemist licences suspended and 22 cancelled**, 54 cases instituted (35 convictions) and 650 under trial. The state restricted stocking of 8 formulations. It also said there is no regulatory framework for online sale and that "medicines that can be misused for intoxication remain available online", and it raised this with the Union health minister — [Medical Dialogues / PTI, 16 Oct 2026](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/news/industry/pharma/punjab-seizes-rs-2064-crore-worth-of-medicines-suspends-520-chemist-licences-in-drug-law-crackdown-181441).
- **Uttar Pradesh codeine-syrup racket (late 2025):** about 3.5 lakh bottles seized, 32 arrested and an SIT formed — [WION](https://wionews.com/india-news/cough-syrup-racket-busted-in-uttar-pradesh-3-5-lakh-bottles-seized-32-arrested-as-government-forms-sit-to-probe-1765294404967). The ED opened a money-laundering case on about 30 UP Police FIRs, with proceeds of crime of about Rs 1,000 crore. It searched 25 locations in UP, Jharkhand and Gujarat on 12-13 Dec 2025, seized 46,891 bottles in Lucknow, and says syrup was diverted to Nepal and Bangladesh — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/ed-raids-across-three-states-in-cough-syrup-smuggling-case); [WION](https://www.wionews.com/india-news/uttar-pradesh-cough-syrup-case-ed-conducts-raids-at-25-locations-across-three-states-main-accused-on-run-1765518245604/amp). An FIR was filed against a pharmacy owner in Azamgarh during the crackdown — [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/state/fir-against-pharmacy-owner-in-ups-azamgrah-amid-crackdown-on-illicit-cough-syrup-trade-enn25120407254). A search summary also reported FIRs against 128 establishments in 28 districts, but I could not tie that figure to a specific article, so treat it as unverified.
- **Tramadol has been a psychotropic substance under the NDPS Act since S.O. 1761(E), 26 Apr 2018** (Department of Revenue), after NCB raised concerns about diversion. Before that it was only Schedule H — [BioSpectrum](https://biospectrumindia.com/news/22/10849/govt-brings-tramadol-under-narcotics-law-regulation.html); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/india/food-and-drugs-law/701838/government-declares-tramadol-a-psychotropic-substance-to-curb-its-abuse-and-trafficking); [Jawahar Customs PN-073](https://www.jawaharcustoms.gov.in/pdf/PN-2018/PN_073.pdf).
- **Schedule H1:** created by G.S.R. 588(E) of 30 Aug 2013 and in force from 1 Mar 2014 with 46 entries. Sales go in a separate register kept for 3 years and open to inspection, and packs carry a red Rx and a boxed warning — [InsightsonIndia](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2020/03/28/what-is-a-schedule-h1-drug/); [NTEP](https://ntep.in/node/938). The list includes alprazolam, codeine, diazepam, midazolam, nitrazepam, pentazocine, buprenorphine and diphenoxylate. It also includes many cephalosporins, carbapenems and fluoroquinolones, and isoniazid, rifampicin and pyrazinamide — [CSJMU course chapter (secondary)](https://gyansanchay.csjmu.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/chapter4.pdf). Goa's regulator cites the notification as "558(E)", probably a typo — [Goa DFDA](https://dfda.goa.gov.in/?p=4360). A blog lists pregabalin as H1 serial no. 51 — [Laafon (secondary)](https://laafon.com/understanding-the-schedule-h1-drug-list/). An NHSRC template exists for the Schedule H1 register and NDPS forms — [NHSRC](https://www.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2021-07/Attachment%20A%2C%20Schedule%20H1%20register%20and%20NDPS%20Forms.pdf).
- **E-pharmacy exposure:** the 2018 draft e-pharmacy rules would have required CDSCO registration and barred e-pharmacies from supplying narcotic, psychotropic and tranquilliser drugs — [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/893030/health-ministry-releases-draft-rules-to-regulate-online-sale-of-medicines); [Medical Dialogues](https://business.medicaldialogues.in/health-ministry-draft-rules-on-sale-of-drugs-by-e-pharmacy-details). They remain unenacted despite later draft bills and court deadlines — [LSHTM](https://www.lshtm.ac.uk/media/102426). As of Oct 2026, Punjab says there is still no framework (above). On 8 Feb 2023 the DCGI served show-cause notices on about 20 e-pharmacies, including Amazon and Flipkart Health+, for unlicensed sales, with two days to reply — [Tribune](https://tribuneindia.com/news/unlicensed-sales-notices-to-20-e-pharmacies-480446). This followed the Delhi HC Zaheer case, which arose after a patient bought **Schedule X drugs online without a prescription** — [IJME 2025 commentary](https://ijme.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/COMMENTARY-Haripriya-1.pdf); [IBA](https://www.ibanet.org/e-pharmacies-and-the-law-an-uneasy-balance).
- **Telemedicine Practice Guidelines 2020:** List O (OTC, any mode), List A (first prescription needs video; refills allowed), List B (add-ons only at follow-up for the same condition), and a prohibited list (Schedule X and NDPS drugs) that cannot be prescribed by teleconsultation — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/Telemedicine-Practice-Guidelines); [Factly](https://factly.in/explainer-what-is-in-the-new-telemedicine-guidelines-notified-by-the-government); [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8106416). Sources differ on whether the prohibited category is called "List C".
- **Online diversion precedent:** in 2016 NCB raided an Indian online-pharmacy operation shipping alprazolam abroad and found about 800,000 tablets — [Pharmacy Daily](https://pharmacydaily.com.au/news/indian-scam-busted/58258). In May 2026 NCB Hyderabad arrested three people, including a freelance chemist, in an illegal alprazolam manufacturing case. The listing also shows a Drug Controller warning to pharmacies on Schedule H, H1 and X drugs; I saw headlines only — [Medical Dialogues topic page](https://medicaldialogues.in/topics/alprazolam).
- **Data seed:** the Kaggle "Extensive A-Z" India dataset has a habit-forming column (repo `PS_ANALYSIS.md` §3) — [Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/riturajsingh2004/extensive-a-z-medicines-dataset-of-india).

#### B. Antimicrobial stewardship beyond AWaRe (H1 antibiotics, OTC antibiotic sale, duplicates, course length)
- **OTC antibiotic sale is common.** In a 2024-25 simulated-patient study of 200 community pharmacies in Coimbatore, **76% dispensed antibiotics without a prescription** (82% for upper respiratory infection, 70% for diarrhoea) — [IJCMPH](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/view/13853). An earlier Bangalore study (261 pharmacies) measured the same practice and its avoidable cost — [Shet et al., ARIC 2015](https://aricjournal.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s13756-015-0098-8).
- **Kerala (state hook):** Kerala was the first state to officially ban OTC antibiotic sale. Operation AMRITH (Jan 2024) uses surprise inspections, sales records, a mandatory notice, blue envelopes and a toll-free complaint line. The health minister claimed Rs 1,400 crore of unnecessary antibiotic use was curbed and use fell 20-30% in one year. KARS-Net data for 2023: 12% of E. coli and 43% of Klebsiella blood isolates were carbapenem-resistant, and MRSA rose to 33% (27% in 2022) — [The South First, Dec 2024](https://thesouthfirst.com/kerala/keralas-fight-against-antimicrobial-resistance-a-success-story-with-challenges-ahead/); [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/news/health/kerala-comes-up-with-operation-amrith-to-tackle-amr-93865).
- **NAP-AMR 2.0** (2025-2029) was launched on 18 Nov 2025 and emphasises private-sector engagement and stewardship — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/health/waaw-2025-indias-much-awaited-second-version-of-the-national-action-plan-on-amr-released); [Outlook](https://www.outlookindia.com/amp/story/healthcare-spotlight/govt-launches-national-action-plan-on-amr-20-calls-for-collective-action-amid-rising-antibiotic-misuse). One secondary source says Kerala and Gujarat were the first states to ban OTC antibiotics — [ClearIAS](https://www.clearias.com/national-action-plan-on-amr/). The search found no national OTC antibiotic ban.
- **ICMR AMRSN 2024** (8th edition) uses tertiary-care data and says it should not be extrapolated to the community — [ICMR PDF](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/Report/1763981012_icmramrsnannualreport2024.pdf). Press reports on earlier editions describe rising carbapenem and fluoroquinolone resistance — [Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/health/various-pathogens-increasingly-becoming-resistant-to-critically-important-antimicrobials-icmr-report-92013).
- Third- and fourth-generation antibiotics are in Schedule H1, so each sale needs a register entry (see A).

#### C. Tuberculosis (anti-TB dispensing duty, NTEP weight bands, rifampicin)
- **Legal duty on chemists:** a 2018 MoHFW notification requires pharmacies and chemists dispensing anti-TB drugs to notify the patient, with medicine details and a prescription copy, to the district nodal officer. Failure could attract IPC s.269/270 (up to 6 months and up to 2 years) — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/attention-doctors-chemists-notify-tb-or-go-to-jail); [PharmaTutor](https://www.pharmatutor.org/pharma-news/2018/central-government-strengthens-tb-cure-by-making-obligatory-action-from-doctors-and-pharmacists-to-submit-complete-info-on-tuberculosis-patients). Anti-TB drugs were placed in Schedule H1 in 2013, and Nikshay is open to chemists for notification — [NTEP](https://ntep.in/node/938). Kerala used H1 registers to find missing private-sector TB cases — [GHSP](https://www.ghspjournal.org/node/5021).
- **Compliance gap:** in 2022, fewer than 5% of registered pharmacists reported under NTEP — [The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article_pdf/health/india-cannot-eliminate-tb-without-addressing-the-taboo-hurdle). This comes from one article, so verify before quoting on a slide.
- **Scale:** 26.07 lakh TB cases were notified in 2024 and 25.5 lakh in 2023, the highest ever — [MoHFW press release](https://mohfw.gov.in/press-info/8566). The private sector contributed about 32% (over 8.4 lakh) in 2023 — [Medical Dialogues, India TB Report 2024](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/health/india-tb-report-2024-16-percent-decline-in-new-cases-since-2015-126428). A Parliament reply gives 9.32 lakh private notifications in 2023 — [Lok Sabha AS361](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2987188/1/AS361_baQkeh.pdf). The two figures conflict.
- **NTEP weight-band dosing (free, official, machine-encodable):** adult HRZE 4FDC (H75/R150/Z400/E275) is 2 tablets for 25-34 kg, 3 for 35-49, 4 for 50-64, 5 for 65-75 and 6 for 75 kg or more. The schedule is 8 weeks intensive (HRZE) and 16 weeks continuation (HRE). The dose is recalculated if weight changes by about 5 kg across a band — [NTEP node 770](https://ntep.in/node/770). Other weight-band tables (e.g. AAC 2020, trial protocols) differ and must not be mixed with the NTEP table — [PMC table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7038290/table/T4).

#### D. Chronic disease programmes (diabetes, hypertension, HIV, epilepsy, thyroid, anticoagulation)
- **Size of the problem:** ICMR-INDIAB (Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology, June 2023) estimated 101 million people with diabetes (11.4%), 136 million with prediabetes and 315 million with hypertension (35.5%) in 2021, from a survey of 113,043 people across 31 states/UTs — [Morung Express/PTI](https://morungexpress.com/315-mn-indians-suffer-from-hypertension-101-mn-are-diabetic-icmr-study); [National Herald](https://www.nationalheraldindia.com/science-and-tech/over-11-per-cent-indians-diabetic-36-per-cent-have-hypertension-lancet-survey-shows).
- **Sulfonylurea hypoglycaemia:** it was the most common ADR among oral antidiabetic users in an Indian tertiary-care study, with comorbidity and polypharmacy as risk factors — [Joshi et al., JCDR 2022](https://www.jcdr.net/articles/PDF/16401/55130_CE_F[SK]_PF1(SM_SS)_PFA(SM_KM)_PN(KM).pdf). In the Madurai survey, at least 20% of 100 patients had a hospital admission for hypoglycaemia since diagnosis, and about 60% said their doctor never discussed it — [BJD 2015](https://bjd-abcd.com/index.php/bjd/article/view/48). Long-acting sulfonylureas carry higher risk in older people and in renal impairment — [MJA 2004](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2004/180/2/long-acting-sulfonylureas-long-acting-hypoglycaemia).
- Oral hypoglycaemics are Telemedicine List A (refills allowed routinely) — [Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/Telemedicine-Practice-Guidelines). So refills often arrive at e-pharmacies without a fresh physical visit (inference from the rule).
- HIV ART (NACO), epilepsy, thyroid and warfarin/acenocoumarol INR: I searched for none of these specifically and have no India-specific harm numbers (see Gaps).

#### E. "Stacking" risks (QT, serotonin, hyperkalaemia, bleeding, hypoglycaemia)
- **QT/TdP stacking is frequent in Indian prescriptions.** At AIIMS Rishikesh, 636 of 1,326 psychiatry outpatients (47.9%) received interacting drugs that can cause torsades — [PubMed 31053339](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/31053339). Among 208 patients aged 60 and over, 147 (70.7%) were on such agents, with 288 torsadogenic interacting pairs — [J Family Med Prim Care, Jan 2020](https://doaj.org/article/0c0ba928a68d453783f0a9bba841fcd6). In a South Indian critical-care study, azithromycin, clarithromycin, ondansetron and amiodarone were the most prescribed known-risk QT drugs — [Manipal](https://researcher.manipal.edu/en/publications/prescribing-practices-of-qt-interval-prolonging-drugs-in-critical/). Azithromycin plus cetirizine for upper respiratory infection produced a significant QTc change by day 4 — [JCDR](https://www.jcdr.net/articles/PDF/10573/26525_CE(RA1)_F(T)PF1_(NE_MJ_PY)_PFA(VJ_SS).pdf).
- **QT data licensing:** CredibleMeds says commercial use of the QTdrugs list is prohibited without an AZCERT licence. Embedding tiers are listed at USD 20,000-30,000 a year plus API fees. Government, academic, non-profit and self-employed providers may use it non-commercially. These figures came from mirror/dev pages, so check the live page — [CredibleMeds licences](https://crediblemeds.org/licenses/license1); [CredibleMeds API](https://api.crediblemeds.org/).
- I did not search for India-specific harm numbers on serotonin, hyperkalaemia or bleeding stacking (see Gaps).

#### F. Drug-disease contraindications (problem list, or conditions inferred from medicines)
- Incumbents already sell this: eMIMSplus (India edition) flags "medicines that may be unsuitable due to a pre-existing health condition" — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6745163411). Medi-Span's Drug Disease Contraindications module covers disease states "or known genetic factors" — [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/solutions/medi-span/medi-span/clinical-screening).
- I found no India-specific harm study on drug-disease conflicts in this search (see Gaps).

#### G. Lab values beyond creatinine, and pharmacogenomics
- **HLA-B\*15:02 and carbamazepine:** a meta-analysis of Indians (Malaysia and India) gave OR 38.5 (95% CI 6.8-217) for SJS/TEN, but from very few cases (2 of 5 Indian cases carried the allele) — [Khor et al., PubMed 25266342](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25266342). Allele frequency was about 1.2% in 5,469 North Indians — [Vaccine Research 2022](https://vacres.pasteur.ac.ir/article-1-304-en.html). Pre-emptive genotyping is not practised in India, and carbamazepine is among the most common causes of SJS/TEN in India — [Frontiers in Genetics 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/genetics/articles/10.3389/fgene.2020.607532/epub).
- **CYP2C19 and clopidogrel:** the \*2 allele frequency was 0.375 in Asian Indians (Singapore) — [Duke Scholars](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/759589). 50% of 60 North Indian PCI patients carried a loss-of-function allele — [Cureus/PMC10439363](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10439363/). In Kerala, the \*2 frequency was 41% in 60 ACS patients — [JCDR](https://jcdr.net/ReadXMLFile.aspx?id=15524). These are small, single-centre studies.
- Lexicomp has added fields saying whether gene testing is required, recommended or to be considered — [Upstate library blog](https://blogs.upstate.edu/library/?p=3723).

#### H. Price, substitution and supply (NSQ, recalls, NPPA, Jan Aushadhi, cold chain)
- **Coldrif (Oct 2025):** at least 20 children died in Madhya Pradesh after a cough syrup with diethylene glycol at nearly 500 times the limit. CDSCO recommended cancelling the manufacturer's licence, and MP, Tamil Nadu, Odisha and Puducherry banned and recalled the product — [KATV/Sinclair wire](https://katv.com/news/nation-world/at-least-20-children-die-after-ingesting-contaminated-cough-syrup-reports-india-madhya-pradesh-medicine-toxins-diethylene-glycol-deg-death-investigation-arrest-charges-drugs-pharmaceuticals). More than 700 manufacturers were under scrutiny by Dec 2025 — [IANS](https://ianslive.in/cough-syrup-deaths-over-700-manufacturers-under-intense-scrutiny-says-anupriya-patel--20251202154211). Parchi already ingests NSQ lists. Coldrif shows that **state-level bans and recalls** need to be ingested too.
- Punjab also complained about price differences between products with the same active ingredient and said the Centre had not acted "until recent Supreme Court directions" — [Medical Dialogues / PTI](https://medicaldialogues.in/amp/news/industry/pharma/punjab-seizes-rs-2064-crore-worth-of-medicines-suspends-520-chemist-licences-in-drug-law-crackdown-181441).
- Cold chain (insulin delivery by e-pharmacies): not researched (see Gaps).

#### I. OTC-only and no-Rx carts
- The 76% no-prescription antibiotic dispensing rate (Coimbatore) shows that prescription-only items routinely leave pharmacies without a prescription — [IJCMPH](https://www.ijcmph.com/index.php/ijcmph/article/view/13853). Self-prescribing of antibiotics was common in a 6-ED study in Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Delhi — [PMC6824172](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824172).

#### J. Pharmacist workflow and compliance (Rx validity, prescriber registration, telemedicine lists, e-pharmacy rules)
- Prescriber registration can be checked against the NMC Indian Medical Register or State Medical Council registers — [MedBound Times](https://www.medboundtimes.com/medicine/verify-doctor-registration-india). Commercial NMC-verification APIs exist (e.g. Decentro returns name, registration number, qualification and status) — [Decentro](https://decentro.tech/api-hub/nmc-verification-api). The pan-India National Medical Register portal was still being built, with 1.66 lakh doctors registered at the time of reporting — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/health/pan-india-portal-for-doctors-registration-likely-to-be-ready-by-next-yr-1-66l-registered-on-nmr-so-far/3059927/).
- E-pharmacy regulation is still the unenacted 2018 draft (see A). The DCGI's 2023 notices said Schedule H, H1 and X sales are allowed only on a valid prescription from a registered practitioner and under a registered pharmacist's supervision — [Tribune](https://tribuneindia.com/news/unlicensed-sales-notices-to-20-e-pharmacies-480446); [Sentinel Assam](https://www.sentinelassam.com/more-news/national-news/dcgi-sends-notice-to-e-pharmacies-for-selling-drugs-without-licence-637202).

Price/NPPA, cold chain, recalls** | Recall harm strong (Coldrif) but already covered by NSQ | NPPA ceiling prices; state recalls | Price: margin conflict (repo §9.4); recalls: high | NPPA lists; state recall notices are scattered | Recalls fit (batch flag); price is a non-safety feature | Recalls 2-4 h (extend NSQ list); price and cold chain are distractions |
| **Food/alcohol/fasting; veterinary** | Not researched / no evidence | None | Low | None | Belongs in the leaflet (`api/leaflet`), not the alert engine | Distraction |

Other inferences:
- **Schedule H1 is the unifying frame.** One "regulated-drug layer" tags the three H1 groups (habit-forming, 3rd/4th-gen antibiotics, anti-TB) plus Schedule X and NDPS. That single layer powers domains A, B, C, I and J. Each sale generates the register line the law requires, and the timing is good: pregabalin H1 starts 18 days after today (12 Nov 2026).
- **Phase 2 history makes early-refill detection cheap and unique.** The timeline already computes overlapping supply windows per ingredient and names doctors and dates. An "early refill / overlapping supply of a controlled ingredient from more than one prescriber" rule reuses this logic. The existing 7-day refill grace should be tightened, or turned off, for H1/NDPS ingredients. It must be worded as "informs the pharmacist" (e.g. "Third alprazolam supply in 30 days from 2 prescribers; check the prescriptions") and never as an accusation of the customer.
- E-pharmacies often bundle teleconsultations. A rule saying "NDPS/Schedule X drug on a prescription marked teleconsultation" (Telemedicine prohibited list) is a precise, deterministic, legally grounded Interrupt. It needs a new optional `consult_mode` field on `Prescription` (additive contract change).
- The TB notification flag is a rare case where the alert is a **legal duty** and not a judgement call, so it suits the "informs, never decides" rule well. The weight-band check needs patient weight, which Parchi already prompts for (ask-only-when-needed).
- None of these checks require an LLM, which keeps the deterministic, offline design intact.

### Gaps
- Tramadol and zolpidem membership of Schedule H1 could not be confirmed (tramadol is NDPS-psychotropic). The full official H1 list of 46 should be taken from the Gazette (G.S.R. 588(E)) before coding. I found no source for which codeine cough-syrup strengths fall under NDPS exemptions.
- The current BNS (Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita) sections replacing IPC s.269/270 for the TB notification duty were not verified. Whether the 2018 TB notification is still being enforced in 2026 was not verified.
- I found no India data on doctor shopping or early refills (e.g. prescription-monitoring studies). The harm case rests on enforcement news, not prevalence.
- There is no 2025 or 2026 enforcement case specifically about an e-pharmacy selling H1/NDPS drugs. Punjab's Oct 2026 statement is general, and the DCGI notices date from 2023.
- I found no India-specific harm numbers for serotonin, hyperkalaemia or bleeding stacking, drug-disease conflicts, food/alcohol/fasting interactions, HIV ART interactions, warfarin/acenocoumarol INR, or insulin cold-chain failures.
- The ICMR AMRSN 2024 carbapenem percentages were not extracted (only the report's opening pages loaded). Kerala's 20-30% fall in antibiotic use is a minister's claim, not an audited figure.
- CredibleMeds prices come from mirror/dev pages. I did not confirm whether a hackathon demo counts as "non-commercial".
- Whether e-pharmacy OTC-only carts get pharmacist review at all is not public.

## Key Question 3: What do competitors already cover in these domains?

### Takeaway
Paid incumbents (MIMS/eMIMSplus, Wolters Kluwer Medi-Span/Lexicomp) already cover drug-disease, allergy, CYP450, dose-range, duplicate therapy and PGx fields, so Parchi should not compete there. I found **no evidence** that any competitor offers India-specific regulated-drug compliance: H1 register generation, NDPS/teleconsult checks, early-refill or doctor-shopping detection across a customer's orders, or the TB notification and NTEP weight-band check. That India-law layer is the clearest gap.

### Cited Findings
- **eMIMSplus (MIMS/CIMS India):** 100,000+ drugs approved in India; flags medicines unsuitable because of the allergy profile or a pre-existing condition; CYP450 interactions — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6745163411). Features may vary by country — [MIMS CDS](https://cds.mims.com/emimsplus/).
- **Medi-Span (Wolters Kluwer):** dose screening (age, weight, BSA, renal function, condition; daily, single-dose, frequency, duration and lifetime max), duplicate therapy with a "Duplication Allowance Indicator", drug-disease contraindications including genetic factors, drug-allergy, RxNorm mapping — [Wolters Kluwer](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/solutions/medi-span/medi-span/clinical-screening).
- **Lexicomp:** PGx fields (testing required, recommended or to consider) and allergy-interaction screening — [Upstate library](https://blogs.upstate.edu/library/?p=3723).
- **CredibleMeds:** sells the QT list as a licensed dataset/API — [CredibleMeds](https://crediblemeds.org/licenses/license1).
- **medThinks:** Indian brand combinations, 397,000+ pairs from DDInter + RxNorm + FDA labels, with NLEM/Schedule H/Jan Aushadhi information — [medThinks](https://medthinks.com/drug-interaction-checker-india) (as summarised in repo `PS_ANALYSIS.md` §3).
- **Eka Care:** real-time DDI alerts against the current medication list in its EMR — [Eka Care](https://www.eka.care/) (repo `PS_ANALYSIS.md` §3). **Tata 1mg:** per-drug interaction sections — [1mg](https://www.1mg.com/drugs/dolo-650-tablet-74467) (repo §3). **DrugBank:** commercial DDI API; free checker reportedly retired Mar 2026 (repo §3, ref [4]).
