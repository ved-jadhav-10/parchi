# Self-serve e-pharmacy / chain data onboarding: catalogue and order CSVs mapped to Parchi canonical ingredient IDs (India, as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: research done 25 Oct 2026. Parchi context is taken from the repo (`docs/PLAN.md`, `docs/track-a-data.md`, `src/engine/resolver/catalogue.ts`). Repo facts are cited by file path. Every web fact has an inline link; vendor marketing and secondary write-ups are flagged as such.

## 1. What data do Indian pharmacy ERPs/POS hold and export (Marg, GoFrugal, Logic ERP, RetailGraph, Medica/eMedStore, Busy, Tally)? Is salt/composition usually present?

### Takeaway
Indian pharmacy ERPs do store a salt/generic field, but it is often an optional master that must be switched on. They export it as Excel (.xls/.xlsx) through menu-driven reports rather than a documented CSV schema or API. Expect headers like "Item Name", "Company name", "Salt Name", "MRP", "Rate", "HSN" with free-text salt values. The only mandatory columns are usually name, GST rate and HSN. Parchi's current header matcher misses several of these, including Marg's "Salt Name" and "Company name".

### Cited Findings
**Marg ERP (self-claimed market leader; earlier research found no public API):**
- Marg has a separate **Salt Master** that can be exported to Excel: Masters > Master > Salt Master, then Alt+P, then the Excel button. — [Marg Care: export all Salt](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/inventory-master/38698/1/How-to-export-all-Salt)
- The salt field on items is a **setting that must be enabled**: "How to Enable Salt Option in Item Master" starts at Masters > Marg Setups > Control Room. The article does not say whether it is off by default. — [Marg Care: enable Salt option](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/inventory-master/1715/utils/adler32)
- To export an **item list with salt**, Marg support points to a workaround: Masters > Ease of GST > Item GST%/HSN/SAC/Rates Updation, F10, set Export/Import to Export. The export includes "MRP, Rate, Company name, Salt Name, Purchase Rate, and Discount… etc." The full column list is shown only in screenshots. — [Marg Care: export item list with Salt](https://care.margcompusoft.com/Question.aspx/169430/1/How-to-export-item-list)
- Marg also offers a "Salt Wise List" report (Reports > Other Reports > Item List > Salt Wise List) and stock grouped by salt (Group Type = Salt). — [Marg Care: salt-wise item list](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/other-reports/37214/1/How-to-view-salt-wise)
- Marg supports bulk "Change Salt in All Item" (multi-editing). This suggests salt data is often missing or wrong and gets fixed in bulk. — [Marg Care: change salt in all items](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/multi-editing/34906/1/how-to-change-salt-in-all-item-in-marg-software)
- Marg's documented item-master transfer is Marg-to-Marg, not to an open schema. — [Marg Care: export & import item master Marg to Marg](https://care.margcompusoft.com/margerp/data-import-export/477/1/How-we-can-export-and)

**GoFrugal (suited to chains):**
- Its pharmacy features include an "inbuilt scheduled drug register", "generic name-wise reporting" and a drug index by generic and by brand. So generic name and schedule are stored per item. — [GoFrugal blog: Pharmacy solution highlights](https://www.gofrugal.com/blog/pharmacy-solution-highlights)
- Items can be searched by generic name at billing (Sales Bill, Ctrl+F11, Generic). — [GoFrugal community KB](https://community.gofrugal.com/portal/en/kb/articles/search-and-load-items-using-pharmacy-generic-name)
- A "Master Import Tool" exists for migrating all masters. Its column template is published only as images, so I could not read the column names. — [GoFrugal KB: Master Import Tool](https://community.gofrugal.com/portal/en/kb/articles/all-masters)

**Logic ERP:**
- Item master import accepts .xls/.xlsx: pick a sheet and columns, then map each Excel column to a software field in "External Column Mapping" via the "Field Expression" column. The Item Code is generated by an expression, not imported. Post-import checks mention Item Name, Company Name, Shade/Grouping, GST Details, Sizes and Rates. No salt or composition field is mentioned. — [Logic ERP KB: Import Item Master from Excel](https://kb.logicerp.com/logic-erp-learning-hub-tutorials-and-best-practices/utilities/import-masters-transactions-from-external-files/import-masters-from-external-file/import-item-master-from-excel)
- HSN is handled as "Harmonious Codes/Description" that are set up and then allocated to items. — [Logic ERP KB: Setup Harmonious Codes](https://kb.logicerp.com/logic-erp-learning-hub-tutorials-and-best-practices/setups/setup-items/setup-harmonious-codes-description)

**Other Indian SME ERPs (pattern):**
- Uprow's product import template has only four mandatory fields: Item Name, Item type, GST rate and HSN. Everything else is optional. — [Uprow ERP KB](https://uprowerp.zohodesk.in/portal/en/kb/articles/importing-products-via-excel-files)

**HSN/GST (present in every Indian item master, but not a drug identifier):**
- HSN heading 3004 covers medicaments in measured doses or retail packing. Most formulations moved to 5% GST from 22 Sep 2025 under "GST 2.0". — [Tax Garden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/gst-on-medicines-pharmaceutical-products-india-rates-hsn-2026); [Tally: HSN 3004](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/hsn-code-3004-product-classification-gst-rate-business-filing-guide/)
- Some life-saving drugs went to nil GST. Sources disagree on the count: 33 + 3 per Tally, 36 per Tax Garden. — [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/hsn-code-3004-product-classification-gst-rate-business-filing-guide/); [Tax Garden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/gst-on-medicines-pharmaceutical-products-india-rates-hsn-2026)
- Billing systems set up before Sept 2025 may still carry old rates. Some guides still list 3004 at 18%, which is stale. — [Accountune](https://accountune.com/medicine-gst-rate-list); [Kanakkupillai (outdated)](https://kanakkupillai.com/learn/gst-on-medicines-gst-rate-and-hsn-code-for-pharma-products)

**e-pharmacy catalogues:**
- Tata 1mg-style catalogues already carry a per-SKU salt composition. Parchi's 32k-brand index is built from a 1mg scrape with composition strings. — repo `docs/track-a-data.md`; [junioralive/Indian-Medicine-Dataset](https://github.com/junioralive/Indian-Medicine-Dataset)
- 1MG Technologies contributed its drug corpus to the national Common Drug Codes for India (see Q2). — [NRCeS CDCI release notes, 15 Jan 2025](https://nrces.in/download/files/pdf/doc_SnomedCT-IndiaDrugExtension-PRODUCTION-ReleaseNotes_Current-en-US_IN1000189_20250115.pdf)

**Parchi today (repo):**
- `importCatalogue` matches headers by **exact compacted name** against fixed lists. The composition list is: composition, salt, salts, saltcomposition, generic, genericname, content, contents, ingredients, molecule. The name list includes itemname, product and brand. Price and stock lists exist. There is no manual override. — `src/engine/resolver/catalogue.ts` (HEADERS, `mapColumns`)
- The parser handles `,` `;` and tab CSV with a BOM, but not .xlsx. No fields exist for manufacturer, pack, HSN, schedule or GTIN. — same file

### Inferences
- **Header gaps in Parchi:** Marg's "Salt Name" compacts to `saltname` and "Company name" to `companyname`, and neither is in Parchi's lists. A real Marg export would import with no composition column. The cheapest fix is to add these synonyms plus `mfr`, `manufacturer`, `company`, `pack`, `packing`, `hsn`, `hsncode`, `schedule`, `gtin`, `ean` and `barcode`, and to let the user override the mapping.
- **Excel is the native format:** Marg, Logic and Uprow all export and import Excel, so in-browser .xlsx parsing is a prerequisite for a "self-serve" claim.
- **Map the salt master first:** Marg keeps a separate Salt Master. Mapping a few hundred to a couple thousand salt strings to canonical ids once, then letting thousands of SKUs inherit, is much cheaper than reviewing every SKU. A salt string may carry strengths ("PARACETAMOL 500MG + ..."); this is unverified (see Gaps). The existing `normaliseComposition` and `proposeFromComposition` already handle "A 500mg, B 5mg" text.
- **Salt is a hint, not truth:** Because salt is optional and bulk-edited, an ERP salt column can be wrong. Parchi should keep its current rule: when the catalogue's composition disagrees with the database, the row goes to `review`, not auto-trust.
- **HSN is a sanity check only:** 3004 vs 3003 vs other headings can flag non-drug SKUs (cosmetics, devices) for "ignore". It is useless for ingredient identity.

### Gaps
- I could not read the exact column templates for GoFrugal (image-only KB), Marg's full item export (screenshots), RetailGraph, Medica/eMedStore, Busy or Tally-based pharmacy setups. No public docs were found for the last four. Ask a pilot pharmacy for a real export; that is the fastest way to close this gap.
- I found no source on how consistently Marg/GoFrugal users fill the salt field, or whether salt strings carry strengths. Earlier research's claim that Marg holds >50% share was not re-verified here.
- I found no published e-pharmacy order-export format (order id, customer, doctor, line items) for 1mg, PharmEasy, Apollo 24|7, Netmeds or Truemeds. These are internal systems.

## 2. Which Indian drug master/terminology standards could serve as a join key, and what is their status in 2026?

### Takeaway
India has no NDC-style statutory product code. The strongest join key in 2026 is NRCeS's **Common Drug Codes for India (CDCI)**, a SNOMED CT national extension with ~77.6k branded medicines. It is released quarterly, used in the ABDM FHIR medicine value set, and queryable since June 2026 through the **Bharat Health Terminology Service (BHTS)** FHIR server. GTIN in the Schedule H2 QR codes is a growing pack-level key. Jan Aushadhi codes, ATC and HSN are useful attributes but not brand identifiers. NPPA and CDSCO databases are not usable public product masters.

### Cited Findings
**CDCI (NRCeS / MoHFW, SNOMED CT India Drug Extension):**
- The 15 Jan 2025 production release counts 7,008 suppliers (manufacturers), 58,318 product (trade/brand) names, 743 clinical drugs (generic) and **77,598 real clinical drugs (branded medicines)**. Together with the International Edition that is "9092 Generic and 77598 Branded Medicines". It covers single- and multi-ingredient drugs with up to five active ingredients. — [CDCI release notes 2025-01-15 (PDF)](https://nrces.in/download/files/pdf/doc_SnomedCT-IndiaDrugExtension-PRODUCTION-ReleaseNotes_Current-en-US_IN1000189_20250115.pdf)
- Its stated purpose is common codes "across healthcare records, supply and pharmacy systems". Listed uses include ePrescription, stock management and supply chain. The model follows ISO IDMP (dose form, route, unit of presentation). — same PDF
- Scope: medicines from NLEM 2015 and 2022, PMBJP (Jan Aushadhi), AMRIT, the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines, NACO HIV and COVID-19 protocols. Devices, implants and combi packs are excluded. The corpus came from AIIMS, **1MG Technologies**, C-DAC Noida, RailTel and Pfizer India. — same PDF
- Release cadence: CDCI + Drug Information Service Bundle (DISB) releases came out on 15 Jan, 28 Mar, 20 Jun, 18 Sep and 19 Dec 2025, then 13 Mar, 15 Jun and 31 Aug 2026. That is roughly quarterly. — [NRCeS news](https://www.nrces.in/news)
- There are two packages. The **Terminology Integrated Package** is a SNOMED extension for Affiliate Licensees via MLDS. The **Flat Files Package** is "Tab Separated Values in text files" covering substances, trade/brand names, suppliers, drug forms, generic medicines and branded medicines, under the License.txt shipped in the package. **DISB** is "a set of restful services" for substances, generics and branded medicines, aimed at clinicians, pharmacists and implementers, including allergy/ADR alerts. — [NRCeS national releases](https://www.nrces.in/services/national-releases)
- Licensing: India is a SNOMED International member. Affiliates register on MLDS and accept the Affiliate Licence. Use in member countries carries no affiliate fee, but an annual usage report is required (clause 8.8). — [SNOMED: India member page](https://www.snomed.org/members/india); [NRCeS FAQs](https://nrces.in/faqs); [SNOMED CT Affiliate Licence 2023 (NRCeS)](https://www.nrces.in/download/files/pdf/SNOMED%20CT%20Affiliate%20License%20Agreement%202023.pdf)
- The ABDM FHIR IG v6.5.0 (May 2025) "Medicine Codes" value set includes clinical drugs and **branded medicines from the CDCI** national extension. That is the binding for medications in ABDM FHIR records such as PrescriptionRecord. — [ABDM FHIR IG: Medicine Codes value set](https://nrces.in/ndhm/fhir/r4/ValueSet-ndhm-medicine-codes.html); [NRCeS news (IG v6.5.0, 9 May 2025)](https://www.nrces.in/news)

**BHTS (launched 29 Jun 2026):**
- NRCeS announced "Bharat Health Terminology Service (BHTS) is now available" on 29 Jun 2026, alongside Common Lab Codes for India. — [NRCeS news](https://www.nrces.in/news); [Healthcare IT News](https://www.healthcareitnews.com/news/asia/india-launches-health-terminology-service); [Medical Buyer](https://medicalbuyer.co.in/india-rolls-out-national-health-terminology-service-ending-medical-data-chaos/)
- It is a FHIR R4 terminology service at `https://www.nrces.in/bhts/api/v1/ts/fhir`. Operations: `$lookup`, `$validate-code`, `$subsumes`, `$expand`, `$translate` and `Medication` read. Access is "public and rate limited". Production or high-volume use needs "NRCeS's prior authorization or a local instance". It is meant for use within India, and SNOMED content still needs the Affiliate Licence. This comes from an NHA docs pull request, which is secondary documentation; the page does not mention CDCI explicitly. — [nha-in/docs PR #122](https://github.com/nha-in/docs/pull/122)
- The India Patient Instructions Language Extension (Hindi, Marathi, Tamil, Urdu dosing-instruction terms) had releases in Dec 2025 and Aug 2026. This is relevant to Hinglish sigs. — [NRCeS news](https://www.nrces.in/news)

**No national drug code:**
- I found no official 2025–26 announcement of an Indian "National Drug Code" or "Indian Drug Code". Searches return only the US FDA NDC. — [Wikipedia: National drug code (US)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_drug_code); [FDA NDC Directory](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-approvals-and-databases/national-drug-code-directory)

**Barcode / QR (Schedule H2):**
- G.S.R. 823(E), 17 Nov 2022, requires the top 300 brands in Schedule H2 to carry a barcode or QR code on the primary label (or the secondary label if there is no space). The code holds a unique product identification code, generic name, brand name, manufacturer name and address, batch, manufacturing and expiry dates, and manufacturing licence number. It took effect on **1 Aug 2023**. — [CDSCO FAQ on QR code (21.07.2023)](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadPublic_NoticesFiles/Final%20FAQs%20on%20QR%20code%2021.07.2023.pdf); [GS1 India Implementation Guide](https://www.gs1india.org/wp-content/uploads/Implementation-Guide-1.pdf); [Medical Dialogues: the 300 medicines](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/these-300-medicines-have-been-finalised-for-qr-code-94652)
- The manufacturer chooses the unique product identification code under its own SOP. A commercial guide says it is typically a GTIN. — [CDSCO spurious-drug guidance](https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/Guidance-for-Identification-and-Verification-of-Spurious-Drugs_.pdf); [hello-pharma (commercial)](https://hello-pharma.com/pharma-serialization/india-pharma-barcoding-requirements/)
- **G.S.R. 506(E), 22 Jun 2026** (Drugs (Seventh Amendment) Rules 2026) renumbers the 300-brand list as Table 1 and adds Table 2: all vaccines, antimicrobials, anticancer and NDPS drugs. Compliance is 1 Jul 2027, with antimicrobials reportedly given until 1 Jul 2028. Sources are trade and news write-ups, not the gazette text; one says only "1 July 2027". — [Zelthy](https://www.zelthy.com/blog/schedule-h2-expanded-drug-categories); [Lexplosion](https://lexplosion.in/india-expands-schedule-h2-labelling-how-pharma-manufacturers-must-prepare/); [The Hitavada](https://www.thehitavada.com/Encyc/2026/6/26/now-qr-codes-mandatory-on-vaccines-antimicrobials-cancer-drugs.html); [THIP Media](https://www.thip.media/news/govt-mandates-qr-codes-for-vaccines-antimicrobials-cancer-drugs-to-curb-counterfeit-medicines/147936/)
- GS1 India **DataKart** is a brand-owner-populated central product repository, launched in 2016. It holds GTINs, packaging data, descriptions and compliance/regulatory data, and retailers have used it to validate GTINs since 2019. I found no pharma-specific workflow. — [GS1 India DataKart blog](https://www.gs1india.org/blog/category/datakart); [GS1 India: ShopClues item master](https://gs1india.org/blog/shopclues-to-improve-their-google-shopping-ads-and-their-product-item-master-with-datakart)

**Other registers:**
- **Jan Aushadhi (PMBJP):** the official annexure lists 2,110 medicines as of 31.12.2025, each with drug code, generic name and unit size. — [PIB annexure (Feb 2026)](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/feb/doc202626781701.pdf). Parchi already ships 1,475 of them as `janaushadhi.json`. — repo `docs/track-a-data.md`
- **NPPA IPDMS:** companies file Forms II, III and V (Form V for all formulations) under DPCO 2013. Uptake was poor: in 2016, 646 of ~5,000 companies had registered. I found no 2025–26 update and no public product download. — [BioSpectrum](https://www.biospectrumindia.com/news/95/7666/nppa-pharma-firms-should-register-for-ipdms.html); [Medical Dialogues](https://business.medicaldialogues.in/nppa-issues-notices-to-drug-cos-for-not-registering-under-dpco-2013)
- **CDSCO/ONDLS:** a single-window licensing portal (C-DAC with CDSCO and the states). A secondary summary says 18 states have adopted it, and physical files stopped being accepted for manufacturing licences and WHO-GMP/COPP from 15 Jul 2025. I found no public licensed-formulation register. — [Vajiram & Ravi (secondary)](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/online-national-drugs-licensing-system-ondls-portal/); [MedPath](https://trial.medpath.com/zh/news/98487b216b57d927/india-mandates-digital-only-applications-for-who-gmp-and-copp-certificates-through-ondls-portal)
- **WHO ATC:** Parchi already stores ATC for its curated ingredients, verified against the WHO ATC/DDD index. — repo `docs/track-a-data.md`

### Inferences
- **CDCI as the closest RxNorm analogue:** CDCI's concept types work like RxNorm's layers. Clinical drug (generic) is like SCD, real clinical drug (branded) like SBD, plus a product name and a supplier. Adding an optional `cdci_code` / `snomed_ct` column to Parchi's brand and catalogue mapping record would turn Parchi's canonical ids into a crosswalk, not a competing standard. It also lines up with the FHIR PrescriptionRecord import, whose medications ABDM binds to these codes.
- **Licence before redistribution:** Do not bundle CDCI data into the public PWA until the Affiliate Licence and the Flat-File License.txt terms are checked for redistribution and sublicensing. Online lookup through BHTS or DISB is the low-risk demo path. Production would ingest the TSV flat files in `data/build.py` behind the licence.
- **GTIN is the future pack-level key:** a GTIN column in the catalogue, scanned from the strip at verification, would let Parchi confirm the brand and line it up with NSQ batch flags. It only covers H2 brands now and the Table 2 categories from 2027–28. The code itself is manufacturer-chosen, so not every QR will carry a GS1 GTIN.
- **HSN, Jan Aushadhi codes and ATC are attributes, not keys:** HSN is a 4–8 digit tariff heading. Jan Aushadhi codes cover only ~2.1k generics. ATC is ingredient-level and has no codes for many Indian FDCs.

### Gaps
- The CDCI Flat-File License.txt terms could not be read (they ship inside the package). It is unclear whether the 2026 releases grew beyond 77.6k branded concepts; I only extracted counts for Jan 2025.
- BHTS's exact rate limits, and whether it serves CDCI branded medicines through `$lookup`/`Medication` read, are unconfirmed. The source is a docs pull request, not NRCeS API docs.
- I found no official gazette text for G.S.R. 506(E). The antimicrobials date (2027 vs 2028) needs checking at egazette.gov.in.
- I found no evidence of a CDSCO public brand/product master or an ABDM "drug registry" beyond CDCI.

## 3. Best-practice UX for self-serve CSV import and entity mapping

### Takeaway
Mature importers share one flow: upload, then **map columns** (auto-detected, overridable, remembered per tenant), then **validate and correct** in bulk, then a **pre-commit summary**. Terminology tools such as Usagi add the entity-mapping layer: a pre-computed best match with a score, a human review queue with explicit statuses and author, bulk approve, and reuse of old mappings when the source list changes. For Indian FDC-heavy catalogues, plain string-similarity mapping is known to fail on combinations and concentrations. Parchi's ingredient-set plus strength comparison is the right core, wrapped in this UX.

### Cited Findings
- **Flatfile** has three stages: add data, match headers to the output template, then validate and correct. — [Flatfile: import overview](https://support.flatfile.com/articles/7763163677-importing-data-with-flatfile-overview)
- **Flatfile mapping memory** remembers previously chosen mappings and suggests them for similar schemas. History is kept per environment and per schema, and is saved only once a file is fully mapped. Newer choices replace older history, and history can be deleted on demand. The company claims its ML plus memory predicts more than 90% of matching actions (vendor claim). — [Flatfile: mapping accuracy](https://support.flatfile.com/en/articles/8948941-how-does-flatfile-ensure-mapping-is-as-accurate-as-possible); [Flatfile legacy docs: mapping](https://flatfile.com/docs/legacy-docs/concepts/mapping)
- **OneSchema** matches columns exactly, fuzzily, or from past pairings, and skips the Map Columns pane entirely when every column matches. It shows the end state of the data in a modal before import and can allow partially clean imports. — [OneSchema blog (vendor)](https://www.oneschema.co/blog/customizations-dashboard). A competitor's comparison credits it with 50+ prebuilt validations and bulk fixes (find & replace, auto-fix). — [CSVBox (competitor)](https://blog.csvbox.io/oneschema-alternatives-best-tools-for-csv-import/)
- **OHDSI Usagi** builds an initial full mapping by term similarity (Apache Lucene over concept names and synonyms). Users then filter by vocabulary, domain or class, review interactively, and export as `source_to_concept_map`. It can "Apply an old mapping to an updated set of source codes". — [Usagi README](https://github.com/OHDSI/Usagi/blob/master/README.md)
- Usagi's documentation says the mappings "need to be manually reviewed", preferably by someone experienced in the coding system, and that it proposes only standard concepts. — [Usagi docs](https://ohdsi.github.io/Usagi/)
- Usagi has a built-in approval function. v1.4.3 asks who the author is, there is bulk "Edit > Approve selected", and teams use a 4-eyes reviewer principle. There is no single standard QC process; one suggestion is 3 annotators with inter-annotator agreement. — [OHDSI forum: Usagi experience](https://forums.ohdsi.org/t/usagi-experience/1372?page=2); [OHDSI forum: mapping QC](https://forums.ohdsi.org/t/quality-of-mapping-process-peer-review-on-vocabulary-mapping/16813)
- A Thai drug-mapping project (TMT to RxNorm) found Usagi tends to fail on **IV medications with varied concentrations, combination medications and vaccines**. — [OHDSI 2025 slides, Chaisutyakorn](https://ohdsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/03_Krittaphas-Chaisutyakorn_TMT-to-RxNorm.pdf)
- **FHIR R4 ConceptMap equivalence** codes: relatedto, equivalent, equal, wider, subsumes, narrower, specializes, inexact, unmatched and disjoint. For "narrower" and "inexact" the spec says the reason should be described in comments and care is needed in operational use. — [HL7 FHIR R4 ConceptMapEquivalence](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/valueset-concept-map-equivalence.html)
- **Volume skew:** across 7 US pharmacy sources, 1.4–4.4% of product codes made up 50% of prescription volume, and 10.7–34.5% made up 90%. Drug knowledge base (DKB) mapping tables covered 93.0–99.8% of outpatient product codes. Locally "invented codes" explained 36–94% of inpatient non-coverage. The authors concluded outpatient codes are covered "sufficiently well to permit automatic mapping". — [Simonaitis & McDonald, Am J Health-Syst Pharm 2009;66:1743-53](https://www.lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2010034.pdf)
- **Parchi today:** statuses are `confirmed`, `review` and `unmatched`. `review[]` is ranked banned FDC > disagreement > unmatched > look-alike > other. `columns` reports the header mapping. `confirmItem` sets `pharmacist_confirmed`. Alternatives use only confirmed entries. — repo `docs/track-a-data.md`, `src/engine/resolver/catalogue.ts`

### Inferences
Recommended flow for Parchi. Each step lists evidence of need, the pipeline hook, effort, risks and pitch value.
1. **Column mapper with presets and memory.**
   - Need: Marg uses "Salt Name" and "Company name"; Logic requires manual column mapping; Flatfile and OneSchema memory.
   - Hook: change `mapColumns` to return ranked candidates per field (synonym, then fuzzy header, then content sniffing such as "mg" or "+" patterns meaning composition), and accept a user override map. Save a `MappingProfile {tenant, source: 'marg'|'gofrugal'|'logic'|'custom', headers→field}` in IndexedDB. Add optional fields: manufacturer, pack, hsn, schedule, gtin, and ignore/PII.
   - Effort: 3–5 h for the hackathon; vendor presets need real exports.
   - Risks: a wrong mapping looks fine; show 5 sample rows per mapped field before commit.
   - Pitch: "drop your Marg export, two clicks".
2. **.xlsx support in the browser.**
   - Need: Marg, Logic and Uprow are Excel-native.
   - Hook: convert the first sheet to rows, then `importCatalogue`.
   - Effort: 1–2 h.
   - Risks: the SheetJS distribution moved off the npm registry; pin a vetted version and keep the bundle under the PWA precache limit, or lazy-load it.
3. **Map salts first, then SKUs.**
   - Need: Marg's separate Salt Master; volume skew.
   - Hook: a per-tenant alias layer `{tenant_salt_text → canonical ids + mg}` consulted by `proposeFromComposition` after curated aliases. Every SKU sharing that salt string inherits the mapping, but it still passes the database cross-check.
   - Effort: 3–4 h.
   - Risks: salt strings that hide strength or form; FDC combinations (the Usagi failure mode). Always compare the ingredient set and mg, never names alone.
4. **Review queue ranked by risk x volume.**
   - Need: 1.4–4.4% of SKUs make up half the volume.
   - Hook: take `units_sold` or `order_count` from an order CSV or a catalogue "Sales" column. Sort `review[]` by current rank, then volume. Add bulk "accept all exact matches" and "accept all where catalogue salt = database composition", plus keyboard triage.
   - Effort: 2 h.
   - Pitch: "review 200 SKUs, cover 90% of your orders".
5. **Explicit statuses and equivalence.**
   - Map Usagi-style statuses (unchecked / approved / flagged / ignored) onto Parchi's `confirmed` / `review` / `unmatched`. Store a FHIR-style equivalence: equivalent; inexact for a release-type or strength mismatch; unmatched; disjoint for a known look-alike.
   - Hook: additive optional fields on `CatalogueItem`.
   - Effort: 1–2 h.
6. **Re-import diff.**
   - Need: Usagi's "apply old mapping"; Flatfile keeps the newest history.
   - Hook: key by SKU, else normalised name + pack. Classify rows as added, removed, unchanged (approval carries over) or changed (name, salt or strength changed, so approval is dropped and the row returns to review).
   - Effort: 3–4 h.
   - Pitch: "monthly catalogue refresh in minutes, nothing silently re-approved".
7. **LLM assist (online only):**
   - Allowed: suggest header→field mappings and split messy salt strings into a proposal.
   - Rule: output is always `unverified` and goes through `proposeFromComposition` and the database cross-check. It never sets approved, per the Parchi engineering rules.
   - Effort: 2–3 h via `api/`.

### Gaps
- I found no independent UX study of Flatfile vs OneSchema. Their claims are vendor marketing.
- I found no Usagi documentation defining its exact status values (APPROVED/FLAGGED/UNCHECKED). The statuses above are inferred from forum posts.
- I found no Indian-specific catalogue-mapping tool to benchmark against (for example, how 1mg or PharmEasy onboard seller catalogues).

## 4. Governing one buyer's mapping decisions; precedents in pharmacy CDS (FDB, Medi-Span, CIMS, Lexicomp)

### Takeaway
In mature CDS markets the vendor's knowledge base stays read-only. The customer keeps a **local formulary/medication dictionary** that a pharmacist informaticist maps to the vendor's codes. The vendor or integrator supplies crosswalks, and mapping must be complete before screening is trusted. Parchi should copy this pattern: a per-tenant mapping overlay with approved_by, approved_at and versions. Curated and dataset data must never be silently overridden; conflicts go to a Parchi curator queue.

### Cited Findings
- **Epic plus a vendor drug knowledge base:**
  - Epic customers export their formulary list from Rx Admin as CSV. Vendors then auto-map NDCs in that file to their own medication records, tracking one NDC across EMR, dispensing-cabinet and wholesaler identifiers. — [Bluesight](https://bluesight.com/?p=4366)
  - Medi-Span supplies the data behind Epic screening: drug-drug and drug-allergy interactions, RxNorm mapping and duplicate therapy (vendor material). — [Wolters Kluwer PDF](https://assets.contenthub.wolterskluwer.com/api/public/content/3f7f94da30244f81a807f7fad2521020)
- **Mapping is a pharmacist job:** pharmacist-informaticist roles maintain medication dictionaries, NDC mapping, dispense IDs and formulary configuration in Epic Willow, and test medication changes. — [iHirePharmacy posting](https://www.ihirepharmacy.com/jobs/view/522631437); [Built In Boston posting](https://www.builtinboston.com/job/pharmacist-business-analyst-it-remote/10545466)
- **Vendor-supplied crosswalks:** FDB offers a mapping between its proprietary codes and Canada's CCDD, which many systems integrating with PrescribeIT use. — [Canada Health Infoway forum](https://infocentral.infoway-inforoute.ca/en/forum/214-medication-management/5622-seeking-insights-on-ccdd-prescribeit)
- **Mapping must finish before screening is trusted:** when switching drug databases, mapping must be done first because DUR, formulary and decision-support rules trigger on the mapped medications. — [Amazing Charts 12.0 release notes](https://amazingcharts.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/AC-12.0-Release-Notes.pdf)
- **Local codes are where coverage breaks:** inpatient "invented codes" explained 36–94% of drug knowledge base non-coverage, against 0.11–0.21% of volume for outpatient sources. — [Simonaitis & McDonald 2009](https://www.lhncbc.nlm.nih.gov/LHC-publications/PDF/pub2010034.pdf)
- **Terminology programmes use multi-reviewer approval:** Usagi has an approval function with author tracking, and teams apply a 4-eyes principle and merge via git branches. — [OHDSI forum](https://forums.ohdsi.org/t/usagi-experience/1372?page=2)
- **Parchi's existing governance:**
  - Curated rows override the dataset and every clinical number carries `source` + `pharmacist_approved`. Similar names are never auto-merged.
  - The Kaggle diff never writes to `curated/`; a brand changes only when a person checks the pack.
  - `confirmItem` records pharmacist confirmation, and proposals cannot reach dose totals unconfirmed.
  - Sources: repo `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/track-a-data.md`.

### Inferences
- **Layering rule:** the precedence order is curated (pharmacist-verified, pack-checked) > dataset (1mg-derived) > tenant-confirmed mapping > proposal.
  - A tenant mapping can **add** a brand that Parchi does not know, scoped to that tenant only.
  - It can **annotate** an existing brand, for example "our SKU X = brand Y".
  - It can never change a curated brand's composition. A disagreement raises a `tenant_conflict` review and an optional "report to Parchi" entry in a central curator queue. This mirrors drug knowledge bases: vendor content is read-only, local formulary maps sit on top.
- **Mapping record (additive fields):** `{tenant_id, catalogue_version, sku, source_text, salt_text, canonical: [{id, mg, per_volume_ml}], equivalence, status, method: 'exact'|'fuzzy'|'salt_alias'|'llm_proposal'|'manual', score, approved_by, approver_role ('pharmacist'), approved_at, supersedes?, note}`. Export it as CSV and as a FHIR ConceptMap, so it stays vendor-neutral, in line with the CSV/FHIR rule.
- **Sign-off policy:**
  - Exact or curated matches can be bulk-accepted by any operator.
  - Anything fuzzy, any proposal, any composition disagreement, banned FDCs and LASA cases need a named pharmacist. Production adds a second reviewer for narcotic/H1 or high-volume SKUs.
  - The UI text says "mapped by" and "verified by", never "approved safe", in keeping with the "informs, never decides" rule.
- **Versioning:** each import creates `catalogue_version` N; approvals carry the version and content hash of the row. The re-import diff (Q3 item 6) decides carry-over. Every alert that used a tenant mapping shows "mapping verified by <pharmacist>, <date>" in its source chip.
- **Pitch value:** "your pharmacists' decisions are recorded, versioned and auditable, and never silently overwrite our curated data". This answers the trust question buyers will ask, and mirrors how First Databank and Medi-Span are deployed.

### Gaps
- I found no public documentation on how CIMS India (MIMS), Lexicomp or First Databank/Medi-Span let customers maintain local formulary mappings in India specifically. CIMS India integration terms were not found.
- I found no Indian regulatory guidance on who may approve drug-master mappings in software. That a pharmacist should sign off is an inference from Parchi's own rules and from the e-pharmacy draft's pharmacist-verification requirement (see Q5).

## 5. Security and privacy: DPDP Act 2023 + DPDP Rules 2025 if order CSVs contain patient data; offline processing as a selling point

### Takeaway
Catalogue CSVs carry no personal data. Order CSVs with customer names, phone numbers, doctors or diagnoses do, which makes the e-pharmacy a Data Fiduciary and any hosted Parchi service a Data Processor. The DPDP substantive obligations bite from around 13 May 2027, with penalties up to ₹250 crore for security failures. Processing order files entirely in the pharmacist's browser, with PII columns dropped at mapping time, is a strong and true privacy pitch.

### Cited Findings
- **Timeline:**
  - The DPDP Act was enacted on 11 Aug 2023. The Rules were notified on 14 Nov 2025 with "an eighteen-month period for phased compliance". — [PIB explainer, 17 Nov 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
  - Another source dates the notification G.S.R. 846(E), 13 Nov 2025. — [Wikipedia: DPDP Rules 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Rules,_2025)
  - Substantive obligations apply from **13 May 2027**, with an intermediate milestone in **Nov 2026** (Consent Manager rules). Sources differ slightly on what falls in Nov 2026. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/dpdp-act-and-rules-2025-the-2026-compliance-milestones-businesses-cant-afford-to-miss/1830402); [Hogan Lovells](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-)
  - The Data Protection Board was statutorily set up but not fully staffed as of May 2026, when MeitY invited applications for Chairperson and Members. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/dpdp-act-and-rules-2025-the-2026-compliance-milestones-businesses-cant-afford-to-miss/1830402)
- **Roles and penalties:**
  - A Data Fiduciary decides why and how data is processed. A Data Processor processes personal data on its behalf. — [PIB explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
  - Penalties go up to ₹250 crore for failing to keep reasonable security safeguards, and up to ₹200 crore for failing to notify a breach to the Board and affected people. — same
- **Duties:**
  - Plain-language breach notices go to each affected person. Access, correction and erasure requests must be answered within 90 days.
  - Children's data needs verifiable parental consent "unless the processing relates to essential services such as healthcare".
  - Significant Data Fiduciaries face audits, impact assessments and possible local-storage directions.
  - Source: [PIB explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)
- **Pharmacy chains are in scope:** vendor commentary says every pharmacy chain is a data fiduciary (not authoritative). — [miniOrange (vendor)](https://www.miniorange.com/data-privacy/dpdp/healthcare)
- **e-pharmacy-specific rules:** the 2018 draft e-pharmacy rules (G.S.R. 817(E)) are still not final. They would require the portal to be in India, keep e-pharmacy data in India, and bar disclosure of prescription information to third parties; a registered pharmacist verifies before dispensing. — [Novojuris](https://www.novojuris.com/thought-leadership/draft-rules-for-operation-of-e-pharmacies-in-india.html); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2023/11/02/regulation-of-online-pharmacies-in-india/)
- **Separate CCTV draft:** G.S.R. 791(E), 8 Sep 2026, would require CCTV over prescription-drug sales at retail pharmacies, with footage kept for at least 3 months. The source is a law blog; verify against the gazette. — [Prime Legal blog](https://blog.primelegal.in/draft-drug-rules-2026-cctv-mandate-pharmacies-india/)
- **Parchi's existing rule:** API keys live only in `api/`, and the engine is deterministic and runs offline in the browser. — repo `CLAUDE.md`, `docs/PLAN.md`

### Inferences
- **Catalogue import is privacy-free:** it is product master data with no personal data, so it is the safe first self-serve feature.
- **Order and history CSV import:**
  - In the column mapper, mark name, phone, address, email and ABHA id columns as `PII: ignore` by default. Keep only a tenant-side pseudonymous `customer_ref` plus age, sex, weight and creatinine, which the engine needs.
  - Parse and store in IndexedDB only. Never send rows to `api/`. If LLM parsing is used, send only the single drug line text, with no identifiers, and only after explicit opt-in.
  - Effort: 2–3 h. Pitch: "your customers' data never leaves the verifying pharmacist's device; Parchi is not even your Data Processor for offline use."
- **Hosted `api/check` and future sync:** once Parchi hosts these, it becomes a Data Processor. Production then needs a DPA with each tenant, India-region hosting (matching the e-pharmacy draft's localisation), encryption, access logs, a 72-hour-style breach runbook, and retention and erasure hooks. The effort is months, not hours.
- **Risk:** IndexedDB on shared pharmacy PCs is a local exposure. Add a "clear this customer's data" control and, in production, at-rest encryption with a session passphrase.

### Gaps
- I did not read the Rules' exact clauses on security safeguards (for example, log retention) and breach timing. The 72-hour Board reporting figure appears in secondary sources only; verify in the Rules text at [MeitY](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf).
- I found no authoritative guidance on whether an in-browser tool vendor counts as a Data Processor when no data reaches its servers. That is a legal question.
- A reported nationwide chemists' strike on 20 May 2026 against G.S.R. 817(E) / 220(E) appeared in search summaries, but I could not tie it to a specific primary source.

## 6. What is buildable in hackathon hours vs a production version?

### Takeaway
In about 12–18 dev-hours Parchi can demo a convincing self-serve flow: a real Marg-style Excel export, then auto-mapped columns, salt-level mapping, a risk x volume review queue with bulk accept and pharmacist sign-off, then a versioned mapping export (CSV + FHIR ConceptMap) and a re-import diff, all offline. Multi-user governance, CDCI/BHTS integration, GTIN scanning and DPDP-grade hosting are 2–6 month production items.
