# What is verified, by whom, and what is not (HOLES H19)

Every claim we make about Parchi, with the tier of its evidence (`TEST_PLAN.md` §1: T1 external source, T2 generated from one, T3 self-written, T4 human-reviewed). Re-run: `npm test`. Updated 24 Oct 2026. If a claim is not in this table, do not say it.

| Claim | Evidence | Tier | Result | Not verified |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brand names resolve to the right composition, including unseen brands | Eka Care IndianDrugMCQA, 1,092 Indian brands, MIT (`resolver/external.test.ts`) | T1 | 1.4% confidently wrong of named brands; 50% agreement on composition | Half the disagreements may be differing listings, not errors; no pharmacist has adjudicated them |
| Typos of known brands are found | 74 hand-written typos; 818 generated single-edit typos | T3 / T2 (self-generated) | 100% / 97.9% top-1; 0.12% confidently wrong | Real misspellings from a pharmacist's own prescriptions |
| Our adult maximum doses match an Indian source | National Formulary of India as quoted in Eka NFI MCQA (`limits/nfiDose.test.ts`) | T1 | 5 of 5 comparable drugs agree (ibuprofen, metformin, diclofenac exact; omeprazole, dextromethorphan within) | Only 5 of ~33 limits are comparable; the rest rest on US/EU labels (`DATA_RELIABILITY.md` §2). The questions are derived from NFI 2011, not the formulary text itself |
| The ONC high-priority interactions are caught and stop the pharmacist | Phansalkar 2012 list, prescribed as real Indian brands (`rules/oncEndToEnd.test.ts`); the tiers shown reviewed by an MBBS GP on 24 Oct 2026 (`eval/reviewer_list.md`) | T2 + T4 (GP, not a pharmacist) | 16 of 16 pairs behave as written: 13 Interrupt, 3 documented Inform deviations. Before this test, 3 of 16 interrupted | The ONC class members we tested are examples sold in India; mercaptopurine, pethidine, atazanavir, citalopram, ramelteon have no ingredient id |
| The 54-case benchmark | `eval/gold.json`, labels written by the team | T3 | 0 missed, 1 extra | **Not evidence of accuracy**: same people wrote labels and rules. No pharmacist review. No chatbot scored |
| Clinical scenarios (bleeding, triple whammy, QT, potassium...) | `rules/stack.test.ts`, scenarios from the team | T3 | pass | Tiers are proposals; no clinician has reviewed them |
| Alerts never recommend a dose | Blocklist test over every policy text and the demo alerts (`rules/wording.test.ts`) | T3 | 48 checks pass | A blocklist cannot catch every phrasing; no regulatory opinion sought |
| Engine speed (before Phase 2; not re-measured) | `eval/scale.test.ts`, one laptop | measured | median 0.89 ms, p95 2.7 ms, ~1,100 Rx/s single thread; 1,000-order queue ranked in 1.4 ms | Excludes first load and network; one machine; no phone measured |
| Same input, same output | determinism tests in pipeline, history and API suites | T3 | pass | Not compared with a chatbot (no outputs saved) |
| Works offline | Service worker caches ~3 MB after first load | untested here | n/a | Not tested on a real phone with the network off |
| Pregnancy rules are backed by a source | 17 rules; each has an FDA label or National Formulary of India quote (`rules/pregnancyEvidence.test.ts`); every NFI category X/D drug in our table has a rule or a written reason | T1 | pass: 18 NFI drugs, 16 covered, quinine and urea excluded with reasons | Quotes read from openFDA and a derived NFI question set, not the printed formulary; statin, fluconazole and doxycycline tiers are our reading of label wording; no clinician has reviewed them yet |

## Never claimed
Clinical accuracy. Pharmacist review (none yet). The one human review so far is an MBBS GP (45 years of practice) on 24 Oct 2026, covering the reviewer list only; say exactly that, and that he is not a pharmacist (H25). Anything about ChatGPT or other chatbots until `eval/outputs/` holds a real run. Ayurvedic interaction findings (H26): a starter set with limited evidence.
